# Volume XL Glossary

Definitions for terms used in **Volume XL — ISC2 Certification Tracks**,
alphabetized. See also the [volume index](INDEX.md) and the
[master glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md) for cross-volume terminology.

**AMF (Annual Maintenance Fee)** — The yearly fee an ISC2 member pays to
maintain membership and their credentials; a single combined AMF covers a member
who holds several ISC2 certifications. Used in Chapters 01 and 09.

**Associate of ISC2** — The status granted to someone who passes an ISC2 exam
but has not yet met the experience requirement; they have a defined window (up to
six years for CISSP) to earn it. Used in Chapters 01 and 04.

**ATO (Authorization to Operate)** — The formal, risk-based decision by an
Authorizing Official to permit a system to operate, the outcome of the RMF and
the focus of CGRC. Used in Chapter 07.

**CAT (Computer Adaptive Testing)** — An exam format that adjusts question
difficulty to the candidate's ability, used by CISSP, SSCP, and CC; it typically
means fewer items and a variable length. Used in Chapters 01, 03, and 04.

**CBK (Common Body of Knowledge)** — ISC2's canonical body of knowledge for a
credential; the CISSP CBK is its eight domains. Used in Chapter 04.

**CC (Certified in Cybersecurity)** — ISC2's entry-level credential, requiring no
experience, with a free exam and training under the One Million initiative; five
domains. Used in Chapter 02.

**CCSP (Certified Cloud Security Professional)** — ISC2's cloud-security
credential, co-created with the Cloud Security Alliance; six domains. Used in
Chapter 06.

**CGRC (Certified in Governance, Risk and Compliance)** — ISC2's governance,
risk, and compliance credential for authorizing systems (formerly CAP); seven
domains aligned to the NIST RMF. Used in Chapter 07.

**CISSP (Certified Information Systems Security Professional)** — ISC2's flagship
credential certifying program-level security design and management across eight
domains; requires five years of experience. Used in Chapter 04.

**Code of Ethics (ISC2)** — The four canons every ISC2 member agrees to; a
violation (including using brain dumps) can revoke a credential. Used in Chapters
01 and 09.

**CPE (Continuing Professional Education)** — Credits earned to maintain an ISC2
credential over its three-year cycle, with an annual minimum and a three-year
total. Used in Chapters 01 and 09.

**CSSLP (Certified Secure Software Lifecycle Professional)** — ISC2's credential
for building security into every phase of the software development lifecycle;
eight domains. Used in Chapter 08.

**Endorsement** — The step after passing an ISC2 exam in which an existing member
attests to the candidate's experience before the credential is conferred. Used in
Chapters 01 and 09.

**FIPS 199 categorization** — The impact-level (Low/Moderate/High) categorization
of a system taken as the high-water mark across confidentiality, integrity, and
availability; it scopes the control baseline. Used in Chapter 07.

**IAAA** — Identification, Authentication, Authorization, and Accountability: the
sequence behind every access decision. Used in Chapters 02 and 03.

**ISSAP** — The CISSP architecture concentration (Information Systems Security
Architecture Professional); four domains (2025 outline). Used in Chapter 05.

**ISSEP** — The CISSP engineering concentration (Information Systems Security
Engineering Professional); five domains aligned to systems-security engineering.
Used in Chapter 05.

**ISSMP** — The CISSP management concentration (Information Systems Security
Management Professional); six domains. Used in Chapter 05.

**POA&M (Plan of Action and Milestones)** — The tracked record of a control
weakness, its remediation milestone, and due date, produced during assessment.
Used in Chapter 07.

**RMF (Risk Management Framework)** — NIST SP 800-37's seven-step process
(Prepare, Categorize, Select, Implement, Assess, Authorize, Monitor) that CGRC
and ISSEP apply. Used in Chapters 05 and 07.

**SBOM (Software Bill of Materials)** — A complete inventory of a build's
components and versions, scanned against vulnerability feeds and used to verify
provenance in the software supply chain. Used in Chapter 08.

**Shared-responsibility model** — The division of security duties between a cloud
provider and its customer, shifting across IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS; central to CCSP.
Used in Chapter 06.

**SSCP (Systems Security Certified Practitioner)** — ISC2's hands-on
security-operations credential; seven evenly weighted domains. Used in Chapter
03.

**SSP (System Security Plan)** — The document describing how each control is
implemented for a system, produced during RMF implementation. Used in Chapter 07.

**STRIDE** — A threat-modeling taxonomy (Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation,
Information disclosure, Denial of service, Elevation of privilege) used in secure
design. Used in Chapter 08.
